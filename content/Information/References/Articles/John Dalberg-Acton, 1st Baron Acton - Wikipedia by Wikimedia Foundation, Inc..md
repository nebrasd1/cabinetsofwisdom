### id888652107
> **John Emerich Edward Dalberg-Acton, 1st Baron Acton, 13th Marquess of Groppoli**, [KCVO](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Commander_of_the_Royal_Victorian_Order), [DL](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Lieutenant) (10 January 1834 – 19 June 1902), better known as **Lord Acton**, was an English [Catholic](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) historian, [Liberal](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_(UK)) politician, and writer. A strong advocate for [individual liberty](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_liberty), Acton is best known for his timeless observation on the dangers of [concentrated authority](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism). In an 1887 letter to an Anglican bishop, he famously wrote, "Power tends to corrupt, and absolute power corrupts absolutely,"[[1|1]](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dalberg-Acton,_1st_Baron_Acton/#cite_note-lmcone-1) underscoring his belief that unchecked power poses the greatest threat to [human freedom](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_freedom). His works consistently emphasized the importance of [limiting governmental](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_government) and institutional power in favor of individual rights and personal liberty

### id888651888
> I cannot accept your canon that we are to judge Pope and King unlike other men, with a favourable presumption that they did no wrong. If there is any presumption it is the other way, against the holders of power, increasing as the power increases. Historic responsibility has to make up for the want of legal responsibility. **Power tends to corrupt, and absolute power corrupts absolutely. Great men are almost always bad men, even when they exercise influence and not authority, still more when you superadd the tendency or the certainty of corruption by authority.** There is no worse heresy than that the office sanctifies the holder of it.
