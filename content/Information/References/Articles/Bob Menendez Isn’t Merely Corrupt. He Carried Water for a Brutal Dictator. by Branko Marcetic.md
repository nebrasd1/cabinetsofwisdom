## Highlights
### id601860979
> Consider the Saudi government, which despite — or maybe more accurately, because of — years of [close ties](https://jacobin.com/2017/11/trump-russia-bush-saudi-arabia) with the United States and intimate connections between high-ranking officials on both sides, was directly [complicit](https://jacobin.com/2023/05/we-have-new-evidence-of-saudi-involvement-in-9-11) in the September 11 attacks. Among other things, the monarchy’s extensive [domestic influence operations](https://www.opensecrets.org/news/2022/10/saudi-arabia-ramped-up-foreign-influence-operations-in-the-us-during-bidens-presidency/) have ensured it’s faced little censure for this and other crimes. Former high-ranking US officials cycle into [adviser positions](https://www.washingtonpost.com/investigations/interactive/2022/veterans-us-foreign-jobs-saudi-arabia/) to its government; it invests in powerful [tech firms](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2022/10/28/saudis-kingdom-holding-company-to-maintain-twitter-stake), the [entertainment industry](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/hollywood-saudi-arabia-money-again-1235051906/), and [professional sports](https://jacobin.com/2023/07/saudi-arabia-pga-golf-merger-liv-monarchy-human-rights); it [funds](https://responsiblestatecraft.org/wall-street-journal-saudi-arabia/) influential [think tanks](https://responsiblestatecraft.org/new-york-times-saudi-arabia/) that shape US discourse; and it counts among its [foreign agents](https://responsiblestatecraft.org/2022/08/25/the-revolving-door-from-us-government-service-to-lobbying-for-dictators/) former members of congress, their staff, [executive branch](https://www.law.com/nationallawjournal/2018/09/12/gibson-dunns-ted-olson-registers-as-foreign-agent-for-saudi-arabia/?slreturn=20230826152525) [appointees](https://publicintegrity.org/politics/a-donald-trump-appointee-also-a-saudi-government-lobbyist-is-reassessing-his-roles/), and active partisan [fundraisers](https://theintercept.com/2022/09/22/saudi-arabia-norm-coleman-lobbyist-republicans/), agents that have [donated millions](https://foreignpolicy.com/2021/01/27/saudi-arabia-us-influence-machine-lobbying-khashoggi/) to politicians running for office. This is all okay, presumably, because it’s not remotely covert, but rather out in the open.
