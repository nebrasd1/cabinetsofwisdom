### id757327736
> Monsanto, now owned by Bayer, previously attempted to manipulate the scientific peer-review process. He documented the company’s efforts using internal emails released during a lawsuit, [showing Monsanto used ghostwriters](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0048733321000925) and waged campaigns to influence editorial decisions at academic journals, with the apparent goal of “manipulat[ing] the regulatory process so that it could continue selling a product that the firm’s own research indicated might be dangerous.”

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j53trdb40yf2v5b58kr082ag)

### id757327987
> both the public and regulators tend to trust peer-reviewed studies, and distrust company-sponsored research. That’s why in [one email](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0048733321000925), a Monsanto employee explicitly says the goal of keeping people from the company listed as authors is “to help enhance credibility.” Other emails [show](https://s3.amazonaws.com/edu.psu.libraries.scholarsphere-prod/store/e28194d8b75ad4f7ef62f023f235701f.pdf) selective interpretations of toxicity results, and more concern over preventing follow-up studies than public safety.

### id757328807
> It’s now [considering](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/bayer-weighs-texas-two-step-bankruptcy-filing-over-roundup) whether to use a strategy called the [Texas two-step](https://www.levernews.com/a-corporate-poisoner-two-steps-out-of-its-toxic-liability/). In this legal maneuver, a company splits in two, with one half keeping the assets and the other the liabilities. The latter then files for bankruptcy, forcing people seeking compensation into bankruptcy court, which often results in delayed or lower settlements.
