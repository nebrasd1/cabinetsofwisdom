### id972340526
> The combination of profiteering fertility corporations, an irresponsibly lax regulatory environment, and an incentive structure that enables these “mega-donors” has culminated in a widespread and still mounting social crisis—particularly for donor-conceived people who face risks that range from a lack of accurate medical history to ethical disasters like a heighten risk of accidental incest. An extremely unfortunate consequence of putting the work of creating future generations into the hands of a for-profit industry.

### id972340790
> there is [no entity keeping track](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/donor-family-matters/202402/the-problems-with-donor-sibling-groups-of-50-250-or-more) of these births—donor-conceived births are still voluntarily reported—nor, for that matter, how many banks a single donor is going to. Keep in mind that even the cattle industry has [multiple registries](https://donorsiblingregistry.com/blog/california-cryobank-s-record-keeping) tracking the progeny and genetic material of individual cows to avoid inbreeding and track health concerns; figures are collected and must be reported to the public by [government agencies](https://www.nass.usda.gov/charts_and_maps/milk_production_and_milk_cows/). Humans are equally prone to the same health risks, but have nowhere near the same level of safeguards and oversight.
