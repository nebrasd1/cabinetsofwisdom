### id782768734
> To get rid of their asbestos liabilities, industrial manufacturers create a subsidiary company onto which they [offload](https://www.advisen.com/tools/fpnproc/fpns/articles_new_1/P/364798883.html?rid=364798883&list_id=1) their asbestos-related assets. The manufacturer will also add a large pool of cash to their subsidiary, ranging from [hundreds of millions](https://www.itt.com/newsroom/news-releases/2021/itt-announces-sale-of-subsidiary-holding-legacy-liabilities-to-delticus) to even billions of dollars. A private equity company will then acquire the subsidiary company and its asbestos liabilities, making them responsible for any asbestos claims.

### id782768891
> Though scientists knew about the material’s harmful qualities as [early as the 1920s](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1742940/), asbestos litigation didn’t gain traction [until the 1960s](https://www.asbestos.com/mesothelioma-lawyer/asbestos-litigation/#:~:text=1966%20First%20Asbestos%20Lawsuit%20Filed&text=Attorney%20Ward%20Stephenson%20filed%20the,Products%20and%20Owens%2DCorning%20Fiberglas.). In 1970, Congress classified asbestos as a hazardous [air pollutant](https://www.asbestos.com/mesothelioma-lawyer/legislation/ban/), and soon after, most industries began tapering off the use of the substance.
> Still, it wasn’t until this year that the United States’ Environmental Protection Agency followed in the footsteps of more than fifty other countries and [prohibited](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/biden-harris-administration-finalizes-ban-ongoing-uses-asbestos-protect-people-cancer#:~:text=Biden%2DHarris%20Administration%20finalizes%20ban,people%20from%20cancer%20%7C%20US%20EPA) the ongoing use of asbestos. Meanwhile, asbestos exposure is now linked to more than forty thousand deaths nationwide every year, and even more people bear the cost of serious asbestos exposure annually.

### id782768939
> Starting in [the 2000s](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/berkshire-hathaway-in-7-billion-asbestos-claims-deal), subsidiaries owned by Berkshire Hathaway — a multinational conglomerate led by billionaire investor Warren Buffett — began buying up [billions](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/berkshire-hathaway-in-7-billion-asbestos-claims-deal) [of dollars](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2010/07/15/111615.htm#:~:text=In%202006%2C%20Berkshire%20took%20on,claims%20dating%20from%20the%201980s.) [worth of](https://www.ai-cio.com/news/buffett39s-berkshire-hathaway-assumes-aig39s-asbestos-risk/) asbestos liabilities.
> “The entire operation was driven by the target numbers,” a former claims executive from a Berkshire subsidiary [said](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JEVZEId9ZHs) under oath as part of a lawsuit over the company’s activities. He said he was told by higher-ups to “find a way to avoid paying [asbestos claims].”
> As the firm bought up subsidiary companies, they also [bought up](https://www.ai-cio.com/news/buffett39s-berkshire-hathaway-assumes-aig39s-asbestos-risk/) some of the insurers covering the liabilities, in a complicated scheme that let them squeeze out even more profit from the unfolding catastrophe.
