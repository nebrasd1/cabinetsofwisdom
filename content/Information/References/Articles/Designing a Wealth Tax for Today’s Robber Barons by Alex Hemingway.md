### id905468197

> a global level, a recent [Oxfam](https://www.oxfam.org/en/research/multilaterialism-era-global-oligarchy) report estimates that the richest 1 percent now “own more wealth than 95 percent of humanity.”
> This extreme inequality is corrosive, damaging economies, societies, and democracy.

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jy9phmkyjrktkkwvxanh29x4)

### id905468335

> Investment in [infrastructure](https://www.epi.org/publication/the-potential-macroeconomic-benefits-from-increasing-infrastructure-investment/) like [public transit](https://www.apta.com/wp-content/uploads/APTA-Economic-Impact-Public-Transit-2020.pdf) boosts [growth](https://www.ccmm.ca/documents/memoires/2004_2005/btmm_publictransit_study.pdf), productivity, and incomes. Investment in affordable housing, which eases housing shortages and contributes to lower rents, is not only good for renters but also for businesses that struggle to recruit workers who can’t find affordable homes close to work.

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jy9pm6ernejf5ejyaxkz62mm)

### id905468348

> higher inequality worsens a [wide range](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/article/2024/jul/23/landmark-book-spirit-level-cost-inequality-fifteen-years-later-worse-labour) of health and social outcomes, including life expectancy, infant mortality, rates of mental illness, and social trust.

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jy9pmj5j3tdhqs4egj3n7mw7)

### id905470222

> Extreme inequality is also corrosive to democracy, with a growing body of political science research showing that income and wealth concentration has a [distorting influence](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/perspectives-on-politics/article/testing-theories-of-american-politics-elites-interest-groups-and-average-citizens/62327F513959D0A304D4893B382B992B) on [politics](https://items.ssrc.org/democracy-papers/political-inequality-in-affluent-democracies/) and [policy outcomes](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-polisci-052521-094617).

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jy9ppbfm0tc8ns2f15w6gg3c)

### id905482982

> a well-designed wealth tax must have a comprehensive base, applying to all types of assets equally (rather than exempting certain types of assets such as real estate, which would make tax avoidance by shifting between asset classes easy and likely).

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jy9sa95kn08r71qzn7eqmtf6)

### id905483002

> Second, a wealth tax should be narrowly targeted on the superrich, excluding upper-middle-class households that may find it more onerous to make payments if their largest assets are illiquid.

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jy9sarene95qw2hkx2y5k842)

> Third, an effective wealth tax must make use of extensive third-party reporting of assets particularly from financial institutions, rather than relying too heavily on self-reporting as in the case of some older wealth taxes.
