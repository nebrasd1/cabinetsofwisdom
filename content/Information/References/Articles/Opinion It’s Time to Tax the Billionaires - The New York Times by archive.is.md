### id782759582
> There is a way to make tax dodging less attractive: a global minimum tax. In 2021, more than 130 countries [agreed](https://archive.is/o/ZLb6N/https://www.nytimes.com/2021/10/08/business/oecd-global-minimum-tax.html) to apply a minimum tax rate of 15 percent on the profits of large multinational companies. So no matter where a company parks its profits, it still has to pay at least a baseline amount of tax under the agreement.

✏️ To deal with that blackmail, you have to have everybody on board, so there's nowhere to run to. A global minimum tax.

### id782759687
> One challenge to making a minimum tax work is ensuring broad participation. In the multinational minimum tax agreement, participating countries [are allowed](https://archive.is/o/ZLb6N/https://www.foreignaffairs.com/united-states/global-minimum-tax-lives?check_logged_in=1) to overtax companies from nations that haven’t signed on. This incentivizes every country to join the agreement. The same mechanism should be used for billionaires. For example, if Switzerland refuses to tax the superrich who live there, other countries could tax them on its behalf.
