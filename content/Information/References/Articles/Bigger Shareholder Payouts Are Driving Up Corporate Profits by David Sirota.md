### id895297490

> Federal Reserve [study](https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2025/apr/whats-driving-surge-us-corporate-profits) notes that ever-higher prices have coincided with a sudden increase in corporate profit-taking. Those profit increases are fueled by a quest for dividend payouts to company shareholders, rather than for resources to reinvest in companies

> 76 percent of the growth in corporate profits earned by domestic nonfinancial industries was driven by an increase in the dividends rewarding shareholders” — and just 15 percent of the excess cash was devoted to “invest(ing) in longer-term investment projects.”

### id895297418

> In 2024, corporations paid a record [$1.75 trillion](https://www.janushenderson.com/corporate/press-releases/global-dividends-jumped-to-a-record-1-75-trillion-in-2024/) in dividends, including [$651 billion](https://www.investmentweek.co.uk/news/4410384/dividends-us-companies-reach-usd650bn-global-payouts-hit-records) from US-based companies. Dividend payments [more than](https://cdn.janushenderson.com/webdocs/JHGDI_Edition_45_final_Report.pdf) doubled in the last sixteen years, [prompted by](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~saez/chetty-saezQJE05dividends.pdf) the 2003 George W. Bush [tax cuts](https://www.nber.org/digest/aug04/lower-tax-rates-spurred-dividend-growth) on dividends, which mostly [rewarded](https://www.cbpp.org/research/the-legacy-of-the-2001-and-2003-bush-tax-cuts) superrich shareholders rather than encouraging companies to reinvest.
> A recent example: Stellantis laid off 900 autoworkers, then [announced](https://jacobin.com/2025/04/stellantis-tariffs-jobs-shareholders-automakers) a \$2.2 billion dividend payout.
