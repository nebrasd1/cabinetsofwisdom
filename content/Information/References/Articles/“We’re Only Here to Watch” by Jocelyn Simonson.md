### id583748855

> the performance that the Philadelphia courtwatchers observed when the attorneys and magistrate seemed to carefully consider and argue a case before them might represent an ideal example of public justice. And yet, when the person went to jail after the magistrate set bail, the result did not become “justice”—or at least not the courtwatchers’ idea of justice—simply because the legal language of incarceration became momentarily comprehensible. Instead, the performance highlighted for the courtwatchers the absurdity of a system in which state actors can, on command, recite with feeling the arguments, reinforced over decades, necessary to justify incarceration.

✏️ This is digging a little bit into the idea that, justice isn't served by observing those at play, and without the act of watching, injustice was happening.
Injustice is likely happening regardless, but the act of watching enforced a sense of "performance" on the part of the justice system.
It needs to be kept in mind that the whole system isn't exactly a "just" one in the first place.
