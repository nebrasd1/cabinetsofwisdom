## Highlights
### id1012848802
> Despite the online shopping juggernaut’s best efforts, the ranks of America’s indie bookstores have [swelled by 70 percent](https://www.fastcompany.com/91461983/indie-bookstores-are-making-a-shocking-triumphant-comeback) since 2020. The industry’s trade group, the American Booksellers Association, now counts [3,200 members](https://www.fastcompany.com/91529634/independent-bookstore-day-bookshop-org-founder-on-how-small-retailers-are-taking-on-amazon). As a [handful of Silicon Valley titans](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/markets/big-tech-ai-earnings-alphabet-meta-amazon-microsoft-rcna342796) propel an ever-larger share of [our economy](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/11/22/business/the-ai-boom-economy.html), this surprising turnaround for formerly imperiled booksellers isn’t just unexpected good news—it’s proof Americans have both the desire and the ability to resist Big Tech.

### id1012849076
> Physical media is seeing a similar surge, with [57 million more print books](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/12/30/books/book-sales-trends-2025.html) sold in 2025 than in 2019. Blu-Ray and DVDs [are a hit](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/business/story/2026-02-23/why-gen-z-wants-to-buy-rent-dvds-blu-rays-in-age-of-streaming) with streaming-weary Gen Z, while vinyl sales [crossed a billion dollars](https://www.inc.com/jessica-stillman/taylor-swift-nostalgia-and-screen-fatigue-drove-more-than-1-billion-in-vinyl-sales-last-year/91319854) in the United States for the first time in over 40 years.
