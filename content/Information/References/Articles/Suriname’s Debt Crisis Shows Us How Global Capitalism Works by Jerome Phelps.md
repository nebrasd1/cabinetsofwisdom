### id813772577

> dual reality. In the present, there is a [brutal austerity program](https://www.aljazeera.com/opinions/2023/5/1/its-raining-imf-in-suriname) imposed by the International Monetary Fund (IMF), wreaking the usual havoc on people’s lives. At the same time, politicians assure them that the country has a bright future ahead in which abundant oil revenues will solve all problems and benefit everyone.

### id813772605

> Suriname is an important case study in the way financialized neocolonialism works in the twenty-first century

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jczdqvnhmyt99v7dvpdjkytg)

### id813773242

> Dutch settlers took over coffee, sugar, and cotton plantations from the British in 1667 and established what was arguably the most brutal slave economy in the region.

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jczdxwyb9gsr5tzb6cbff1ef)

### id813773106

> before the country gained its independence from the Netherlands, US commercial interests were transforming the landscape. Vast tracts of forest were [flooded](https://www.goldmanprize.org/recipient/wanze-eduards-hugo-jabini/#recipient-bio), forcing the Maroon Saamaka community from its lands in order to build the Afobaka Dam, which would generate hydroelectric power for the bauxite factory of the Aluminum Company of America

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jczdxfqj858hnzqffxtantpg)

### id813773237

> company packed up and left, having managed to sell the dam back to Suriname. Thanks to unfair deals that doubled electricity prices and left Suriname exposed to swings in commodity markets, the country even [owed](https://assets.nationbuilder.com/eurodad/pages/1997/attachments/original/1611155073/suriname-dam-debt-final.pdf?1611155073) Alcoa more than \$100 million for electricity that was produced using its own natural resources.

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jczdxp8yb72837n05f50sk70)

### id813773092

> Private creditors receive 46 percent of external debt payments from the Global South and own 38 percent of Suriname’s debts.

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jczdwjym0j81kgmkxsjaa3an)

### id813773085

> bondholders have [laid claim to Suriname’s future oil revenues](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-10-24/suriname-offers-to-swap-675-million-bonds-for-oil-linked-debt) through a value-recovery instrument. If all goes according to plan, this will line their pockets with a [staggering windfall](https://www.sovdebtoddities.com/p/surinames-sovereign-restructuring) of 30 percent of future oil revenues, up to a total of £689 million. Meanwhile, Suriname will continue to spend 27 percent of its government revenues on external debt payments over the next five years.

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jczdw5mcbbqbcee9zcattxah)

### id813773080

> the agreement with the bondholders is dependent on Suriname changing the legislation of its sovereign wealth fund by December 2024. Fifty years after Suriname’s official independence from the Netherlands, foreign bodies are once again dictating how Suriname uses its resources and what legislation it should pass. This is the new form of colonialism, using debt to gain access to resources.

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jczdvyq45r7cykxfq1y2fjvk)

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jczdy785sjme4vbb18awrx9z)
