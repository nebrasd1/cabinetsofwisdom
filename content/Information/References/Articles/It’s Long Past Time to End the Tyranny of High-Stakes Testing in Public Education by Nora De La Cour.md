> [Campbell’s Law](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlm_cQwc-9M): the more we base social decision-making on a specific quantitative measure, the more likely it is that that measure will become distorted, ultimately corrupting the processes it’s intended to monitor.

### id547733734

> in the long run, attaching high-stakes, or punishments, to student standardized test scores [does not improve](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/T/bo24695545.html) educational outcomes. Instead, it results in a [host](https://narrow645.rssing.com/chan-36529610/article2.html) [of](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1315126.pdf) [perverse](https://archive.kpcc.org/blogs/education/2013/03/28/13089/report-cheating-on-standardized-tests-in-75-percen/) [consequences](https://www.gse.harvard.edu/news/uk/17/11/when-testing-takes-over), with poor, minority, and disabled kids like Dante experiencing the greatest harms. This last point makes a lot of sense when you consider that [standardized testing](https://jacobin.com/2018/04/against-the-sat-testing-meritocracy-race-class) was [first developed](https://www.nea.org/advocating-for-change/new-from-nea/racist-beginnings-standardized-testing) by eugenicists looking to organize people into racist taxonomies based on perceived ability.

### id547736364

> disabilities are not, contrary to normative discourse, the unfortunate possessions of individual people. Rather, they’re cultural experiences, produced by structures that enable some of us while d&#x69;_&#x73;_&#x61;bling others. There’s nothing per se disabling about needing a wheelchair, for example. Disability shows up in the interaction between that wheelchair and the sidewalks, curbs, buses, and buildings [designed exclusively](https://www.newyorker.com/books/page-turner/when-the-world-isnt-designed-for-our-bodies) for people who walk.
