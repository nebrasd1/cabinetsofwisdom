## Highlights
### id861977656
> The “[Big Five](https://almossawi.com/big-five-publishers/)” publishing houses — Macmillan, Simon & Schuster, Hachette, HarperCollins, and Penguin Random House — wield enormous global power, controlling about 80 percent of the English-language trade publishing market.

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp0mvh139tpd5kv2738t5dkt)

### id861977278
> Around 2002, seeing a market that they hadn’t yet tapped and inspired by the success of conservative publisher Regnery, the top four of the Big Five began to aggressively court the Fox News market. Between 2002 and 2010, each set up a dedicated conservative imprint.

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp0mk4c1w8pde3n5p3xdrpcn)

### id861977319
> In 2002, Random House established [Crown Forum](https://www.randomhousebooks.com/imprint/forum/) (now just Forum) “as a way to inform and contribute to the national dialogue and political discourse.” Forum now boasts that it is “one of the leading publishers of politically conservative authors and points of view.”

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp0mkpca394n7zbjw4c1b0en)

### id861977323
> Penguin set up [Sentinel](https://web.archive.org/web/20170307065533/http:/www.penguin.com/publishers/sentinel/) in 2003, which they describe as “a dedicated conservative imprint within Penguin Group [that] publishes a wide variety of right-of-center books on subjects like politics, history, public policy, culture, religion, and international relations.”

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp0mm00q0mpwmpgn99gx7xrb)

### id861977347
> Hachette set up [Center Street](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/imprint/hachette-nashville/center-street/), whose authors include Donald Trump Jr, Pete Hegseth, Jeanine Pirro, Newt Gingrich, Vivek Ramaswamy, and Kristi Noem, followed in 2005 by Simon & Schuster’s [Threshold](https://web.archive.org/web/20100420184409/http:/imprints.simonandschuster.biz/threshold). The Threshold mission statement explains that it is “proud to have published some of the most influential and controversial political leaders of our time,” including Dick Cheney, Donald Trump, Karl Rove, and John Bolton, not to mention “some of the most exciting and controversial broadcasters in media today,” such as Rush Limbaugh, Glenn Beck, Sean Hannity, Laura Ingraham, and Alex Marlow, editor in chief of Breitbart News Network.

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp0mmr73pxmex1qw3ansx6aq)

### id861977373
> In 2010, HarperCollins tried to claim its share of the market with [Broadside Books](https://www.harpercollins.com/collections/broadside-books?sortBy=HCUSproducts_meta.hc-defined.publishdtimestamp_desc), publishing the likes of Peggy Noonan, Ron DeSantis, Christopher Rufo, Tomi Lahren, Jared Kushner, and Vivek Ramaswamy.

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp0mnc70q5e68qmty5n3dzt5)

### id861977388
> As the founding executive director of Broadside Books put it, they publish books by authors like Ann Coulter and Sean Hannity “whose aim is to whip the Republican base into [a froth](https://nymag.com/nymetro/news/rnc/9676/), and get rich in the process.” Each of the Big Five operates as part of what *New York Times* journalist Jim Rutenberg calls “the [Incitement Industry](https://www.nytimes.com/2018/10/31/business/media/dark-web-conspiracy-theories.html?rref=collection%2Ftimestopic%2FRandom%20House%2C%20Inc.).”

🔗 [View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jp0mnxhjv5zn2g5d309ptatq)
