# Read
- Capital Order
	- Solid foundations need to always be established when discussing things like capitalism
	- What is capitalism
		- Wealth
		- A few owning the means of production
		- Those few using the means of production to make more wealth, through the hiring of wage workers
	- Wage relations
		- Primary social relation in capitalism
		- Selling one's capacity to work to an employer in return for a wage
		- Paying those workers less than the value they provide, in order to generate profit.
		- Being dependent on the market to survive.. without money, we don't have food or housing.
	- What is the role of public (government), private (corps), and people
		- Government stays out of the way of capitalism, but still has the mandate of safeguarding private property and wage relations through the rule of law. And also to bail out capitalism when it needs it. Basically government has to be the aloof parent to its spoiled, entitled rich kid.
		- When government does crack the whip and takes control, more often than not we tend to see the cracks in capitalism's facade (think WWI and pandemic crises).
	- Having awareness of the performative answers vs the true answers
- OxFam report is disturbing as always
- ADL defending Elon Musk is... depressingly typical hypocrisy.
- [Simon Willison blog post about link blogs](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Dec/22/link-blog/)
	- Simon Willison talks about categories of content, and how there are:
		- Things I learned
			- For him, these are software tips and tricks and tactics.
			- Is my equivalent my questions and highlights?
		- Descriptions of my projects
		- Things I've found
			- These are just things that are found online and re-linked basically. A public bookmarking system.
	- He also says:
		- "Sharing interesting links with commentary is a low effort, high value way to contribute to internet life at large." I should try to embrace that, instead of feel like what I want to do is less-than, or just regurgitating stuff.
		- After all, the value is in highlighting someone's effort and work, and adding my own insight to it. We build together.
		- Keep in mind to add people's names as much as possible. Showcase them as well as their work.
	- This is making me think about the difference between category 1 and 3, when it comes to an article for example.
		- Sharing the article itself and giving a summary on it, that's category 3.. something I've found
		- Sharing the highlights and insights and bigger picture stuff to lenses and questions, that's category 1.. something I've learned.

	- Way too many plot points that went nowhere (ex-wife, lydia pills, ghost cops, dead dad hijinx, even dead O'Hara hijinx).. all pretty pointless honestly and hardly serve the plot.
	- Could've been a much tighter movie, but oh well.
	- Also, heavy reliance on the dead lands.. does feel like half the film in reality, other half there, right?
- Snowpiercer is still a great watch, and it's another potentially system villain right? You do have a face to it in Wilford, but the whole thing of tempting Curtis into taking over, but maintaining the system, and how the true answer to the whole situation is to blow it all up.. that's very much a system kind of situation.
	- The cannibalism thing is... I'm not sure if it feels off-putting, odd, or what, but it does feel a bit atonal somehow? In terms of puzzle pieces, it doesn't feel like it quite fits.
	- It does share a similarity with Silo, in that both are acting as closed systems and had to come up with ways to deal with population control. Silo goes for medical treatment, whereas here they go for a more violent approach of using rebellions to quell the numbers (while also acting as a release valve + re-establishment of proper order maybe)? To be fair, I do see that parenthetical as quite pertinent and an interesting use of rebellions, especially if they almost become system-sanctioned. That's actually more insidious than I first considered.
	- Heavy-handed, but not as much as John Q or Don't Look Up. Still, that does feel like a necessary trait for this kind of movie.
- John Q is an excellent movie where the system is the villain. I don't care that it's over the top shlock.. it's really good over the top shlock. People respond to that much better than subtle shit. Take it from a DM, people need heavy-handed hints, and more often than not, it's perfectly fine by them.
	- Also reminds me of the Good Place thing of how they all were so proud of finding Michael's "three hints", and he tells them that he left dozens of hints actually.
	- Anyways, John Q even has a line in there from his buddy, "It feels like, it isn't someone that's out of whack, it's something." As in, the system is out of whack and it's the villain of this whole endeavor.
	- Having a movie where they sit around discussing real life shit, it felt surreal to see a slow moment like that play out so well and so viscerally. 
	- God damn did they pick the absolute perfect kid to play that part. Charming and sweet and a powerhouse every time he shows up.

Andor, Silo, this, etc. there are tons of options and they have important messages for us to take in. I want to learn that storytelling technique to get those points across.
