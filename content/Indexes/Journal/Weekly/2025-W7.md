# Read
- 404 had an article about how "You Can't Post Your Way Out of Fascism" and the idea that all the hot takes and constant commenting on the craziness happening just feeds into what the powers that be want us to do. They want us to be caught up in reacting instead of acting... to post instead of to organize.. to say instead of to do.
	- A casual counter to this would be "but people need to be informed and aware.. these things can't escape notice". That's true, and there is value to being informed, but:
		- I think there is a point where someone has enough information.. there is such a thing as info overload, or being desensitized by it all.
		- I believe there's a difference between education and information. And that ties to the trio verbs of protesting that I appreciate: educating, organizing, agitating.
	- It really is the harder thing to do after all.. to act instead of to post. To go out there in the world and interact with people and lend aid, give, support, provide, etc. You can't do that online, not always, not effectively. Internet is a tool, but it can't be the replacement... it shouldn't be.
		- The unfortunate thing that the internet has been supremely good at is that it has conditioned us to constantly seek new information. We seek it, we share it, we create it.. and receive our dopamine hit. And we're stuck.
	- It's not a criticism of internet as a whole, or even of the fun we have there, goofing around, being creative, etc. That's still valid.. as always, two things can be true: internet can be useful and it can be detrimental. It's a great place to hang, but it cannot be the place to replace the on-the-ground organizing and agitating that's needed when you want to solve real-world problems and challenges.
- Read about Extinction Bursts, a concept in psychology, which refers to an increase in the frequency, intensity, or duration of a behavior when it no longer produces the desired outcome or reinforcement. Easiest example is when you're paying with your card, and you swipe it, and it doesn't work. What do you do? You swipe it a dozen more times, each more angrily than the last, until you stop, take a breath and say "I'll just pay with cash". Same with trying to curb gambling, smoking, and any addictions really.
	- You just have to be aware that it will happen, be consistent, and offer positive alternatives to channel their energy into.
- Read an old jacobin article about socialism and bananas (some fad topic at the time), but I got a good thought out of it.
	- Capitalism claims people have economic democracy in the sense that they can vote for things in the market using their wallet.
	- This does not take into consideration income/wealth inequality, which ensures that a handful of people have an infinitely greater voting power than the rest of the entire globe. That's not economic democracy anymore than those same people have the same "voting" power when it comes to political democracy
	- But, maybe that's one form of what it means to have economic democracy when you're under a socialist system. Goods and services can stay market-based, with all the innovation and choices that entails, but if we remove income/wealth inequality, we ensure every "vote" is equal. That sounds, on the surface, as a better way about things.
